A Husband’s Reading Library

Understand what happened to your wife

If you are trying to make sense of something that initially looked like an affair, a consensual relationship, or simply “bad boundaries,” start here. These resources explain the dynamics of adult clergy sexual abuse: pastoral power, vulnerability, grooming, secrecy, trauma, spiritual injury, institutional betrayal, and recovery.

A suggested path: Begin with When Wolves Wear Shepherds’ Clothing, then read How Clergy Sexual Misconduct Happens. Those two pieces provide a strong foundation for understanding why the clergy-congregant relationship cannot be evaluated like a relationship between peers.

Start here: foundational ACSA reading

When Wolves Wear Shepherds’ Clothing: Helping Women Survive Clergy Sexual Abuse — Diana R. Garland

Why husbands should read it: A foundational article focused specifically on adult women sexually abused by clergy and on helping survivors, their families, and congregations understand the abuse of power and betrayal of sacred trust.

Read the article (PDF)
How Clergy Sexual Misconduct Happens: A Qualitative Study of First-Hand Accounts — Diana R. Garland & Christen Argueta

Why husbands should read it: One of the most useful explanations of how ACSA develops in real life. The study drew on interviews with 46 adults who experienced sexual encounters or relationships with religious leaders and 15 other affected people—including husbands.

Read the Baylor PDF
A National Study of Adult Women Sexually Abused by Clergy — David K. Pooler & Liza Barros-Lane

Why husbands should read it: Gives empirical context for what survivors experience after disclosure. In this 159-person study, fewer than 10% reported receiving help and support from their congregation after reporting, while about half reported being blamed and ignored by people in their congregations.

Read the abstract and study details
The Prevalence of Clergy Sexual Advances Toward Adults in Their Congregations — Mark Chaves & Diana Garland

Why husbands should read it: Helps establish that ACSA is not an isolated phenomenon. Using the 2008 General Social Survey, the researchers estimated that 3.1% of women attending religious services at least monthly had received a sexual advance from a religious leader in their own congregation since age 18.

Read the study

Understand power, grooming, and “consent”

Baylor: What Is Adult Clergy Sexual Abuse?

Why husbands should read it: A concise explanation of spiritual authority and the inherent power imbalance between clergy and congregants. It is a useful first resource when a husband is asking, “But she was an adult—how could this be abuse?”

Read Baylor’s overview
Common ACSA Experiences — Clergy Sexual Misconduct Information & Resources

Why husbands should read it: Walks through patterns that can make a survivor’s behavior confusing from the outside: vulnerability, pastoral influence, manipulation, secrecy, and other recurring dynamics reported in ACSA.

Explore common experiences
CSM Questions & Answers

Why husbands should read it: Addresses practical questions that arise when people are first trying to name what happened, including sexualized attention from a pastor, responsibility, boundaries, and why survivors can feel confused or conflicted.

Read the Q&A
Unchaste Celibates: Clergy Sexual Misconduct against Adults—Expressions, Definitions, and Harms — Stephen de Weger & Jodi Death

Why husbands should read it: A detailed scholarly treatment of clergy sexual misconduct involving adults, including grooming, positional power, manipulated or coerced consent, and harm extending beyond the primary survivor to people around her.

Read the open-access article

Understand trauma, disclosure, and recovery

How They Recover: A Qualitative Study of Female Adult Clergy Sexual Abuse Survivors — David K. Pooler

Why husbands should read it: A recent study focused on recovery. It describes ACSA as producing psychological, spiritual, and relational injuries and can help husbands understand that recovery is often multidimensional rather than a simple matter of “moving on.”

Read the open-access study
Restored Voices Collective — ACSA Media Library

Why husbands should use it: Sometimes survivor stories make the dynamics understandable in a way academic language cannot. This collection includes survivor accounts and expert discussions of ACSA, trauma, institutional betrayal, grooming, and the damaging “affair” framing.

Not the Other Woman — ACSA Information & Resources

Why husbands should explore it: A survivor-oriented collection bringing together many of the central articles, books, organizations, and educational resources in the ACSA field.

A note for husbands: Reading about ACSA may change how you interpret things your wife said or did during the abuse. Understanding grooming and power does not require you to deny your own hurt. Both realities can be held at once: she experienced abuse, and you may have experienced profound secondary trauma, betrayal, loss, anger, and grief. Your healing deserves its own safe place rather than being carried by your wife.

This library is educational, not legal or clinical advice. Inclusion does not imply endorsement of every position taken by a linked organization. When seeking counseling, consider asking specifically about experience with sexual trauma, clergy/spiritual abuse, institutional betrayal, and secondary-survivor spouses.
